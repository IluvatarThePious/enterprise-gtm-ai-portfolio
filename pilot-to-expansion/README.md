# Pilot-to-Expansion Playbook

**A generalized enterprise automation work sample.** A pilot should produce a decision about a larger rollout, with commercial and operational evidence available to the same decision-makers.

Adapted from AI-assisted robotics GTM interview preparation. Company names, private interview statements, specific account rankings, vendor claims, and third-party training materials have been removed. This is a newly generalized portfolio edition, not an implemented client engagement.

## Business problem

An enterprise can approve a small test without agreeing on who will fund expansion, what constitutes success, or whether service teams can support additional sites. A technically successful pilot can therefore fail commercially.

This playbook connects qualification, deployment readiness, measurement, and an explicit expansion decision.

## Workflow

1. **Qualify:** define one repeatable task, its operational owner, baseline, budget path, and deployment constraints.
2. **Contract the decision:** agree scope, commercial terms, measurement, review date, and expansion conditions before launch.
3. **Prove:** compare task-level results with the baseline and record differences in volume, environment, or staffing.
4. **Decide:** expand, revise, or stop using evidence and available delivery capacity.

Start with the [pilot charter](pilot-charter.md), [account qualification rubric](qualification.md), and [manager review cadence](manager-cadence.md).

## What makes this useful

- Operational readiness can stop a deal even if its commercial score looks attractive.
- Released labor capacity is distinct from verified cash savings.
- Scores express prioritization judgments, never a probability of closing.
- A named decision owner and next evidence requirement replace vague next steps.

## Worked example

A fictional facilities operator is considering automation for one recurring task at two sites. Its commercial sponsor wants a rollout this quarter, but the second site's support coverage is unconfirmed. The decision is **revise scope**: validate one site and make the second contingent on service readiness. The team does not declare success based on a demonstration alone.

**Decision memo:** task baseline available; sponsor and decision date identified; support capacity for site two missing; no verified avoided expense; one-site pilot can proceed only after its readiness checks and commercial terms are complete. Expansion remains conditional on measured use, customer-agreed service thresholds, and a supported economic case.
